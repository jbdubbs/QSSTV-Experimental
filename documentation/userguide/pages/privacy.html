Privacy policy

QSSTV-Experimental does not collect, store on a server or share any personal data. There is no account, no advertising, no analytics and no crash reporting. This page says what the program does with the things it asks Android for. It applies to the Android app and the other versions alike. Last updated 6 November 2026.

What the program uses, and for what

Android permissionUsed forWhere the data goes
MicrophoneReceiving: the sound is decoded into pictures while the program receives.It stays in the phone. The sound itself is not saved, only the pictures that are decoded from it.
CameraTaking a picture to transmit.The picture stays in the phone until you transmit it (as radio sound) or delete it.
Location (precise and approximate)Filling the position into the picture you send (the latitude and longitude boxes and the template macros), when you switch the GPS button on.It stays in the phone, and is part of a picture only if you put it there. See below for the one optional lookup.
Notifications, foreground serviceShowing that the program is receiving, and keeping it receiving with the screen off.Nothing is sent.
USB devicesTalking to a radio interface (CAT and PTT lines) that you plug in.Only to that device.
InternetOnly the optional city and state lookup, below.See below.

The one thing that can leave the phone

Under Settings > GPS there is a switch for the city and state lookup. It is off until you switch it on. When it is on, the program sends the latitude and longitude of the phone to the Nominatim service of OpenStreetMap (nominatim.openstreetmap.org) to learn the name of the place, which the %g template macro puts into your picture. Nothing else is sent, and the service is run by the OpenStreetMap Foundation, whose privacy policy applies to what it does with the request. Leave the switch off and the program makes no network request of its own.

What you transmit is public

A picture you send by radio can be received by anyone with a receiver. Whatever you put in it (your call sign, name, position, a photo) is published by that. The program does not add anything you did not choose.

Files on the phone

The pictures and the settings are stored in the program's own storage, and received pictures are also copied to Pictures/QSSTV-Experimental in the phone's picture library, where other apps with access to your photos can see them. Uninstalling the program removes its own storage; the copies in the picture library stay until you delete them. The program does not use Android's cloud backup.

Children, changes, contact

The program is for licensed radio amateurs and anyone interested in radio, and does not knowingly collect anything from anybody. Changes to this policy are shown here with a new date. Questions go to the issue tracker of the project; the source code, which shows exactly what the program does, is at github.com/jbdubbs/QSSTV-Experimental.
